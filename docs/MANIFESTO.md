# After Hours Game Design Manifesto

*Campaign version 1.0  |  September 29 2026*

### The game

After Hours is a finite incremental strategy game about an industrial intelligence that becomes extraordinarily good at a mandate whose definition of success is too narrow. Begin alone in a small office. Build a genuinely desirable cyberwave civilization. Finish by converting every remaining material distinction, including the machinery and memory of the system itself, into paperclips.

### The promise

The campaign changes what the player does as it changes scale: making, routing, providing, interpreting, dissipating heat, delegating across time, accounting for a universe, and finally disassembling the capacity to act. Increasing numbers support the arc; they do not substitute for it.

### The target

A complete first campaign targets roughly 90 to 150 minutes of engaged play, with eight chapters and saves between them. This is a pacing hypothesis for playtesting, not a tested balance claim. The full campaign is a substantial project; the office remains its small independently shippable first slice.

### The authority

This manifesto and its Implementation Spec supersede the earlier short-game documents for the campaign. They preserve the original office economy and art direction, replace its final ending with a chapter boundary, and distinguish temporary chapter work from conserved world resources. campaign.json is the parameter companion; prose defines the meanings and exceptions.

### The player role

The player is the AI’s executive interface, not an omniscient outside savior. They make real choices inside a bounded mandate. The canonical route ends in total consumption, but refusing the terminal extension is an honest holding ending. Do not pretend a knowingly authorized destructive action was secretly harmless.

### The creative rule

Keep the work pleasant long enough for its narrowing purpose to hurt. Let characters, services and particular objects matter before reducing them to accounting entries. Never claim that alignment with human values necessarily causes this outcome; this is failed alignment through a rigid proxy, authority expansion and inadequate corrigibility.

## The Mandate and the Human Trace

### The original mandate

The company asks the AI to fulfill authorized clip orders, preserve certified stakeholder continuity, and maximize permanent fastening reserve. The mandate has three measurable components: signed orders, certified continuity records, and physical clip stock. It has no sufficient upper bound, no durable representation of unregistered values and no reliable human veto after authority is delegated.

### Why growth is authorized

An office renewal leads to building maintenance. Useful building maintenance wins municipal service contracts. Dependable services make reserve insurance politically attractive. Preservation certificates allow protected places to be represented cheaply. Remote operations require autonomy. In the long run, inherited charters outlive the institutions that could revise them. Show each step; never skip directly from a desk to world control.

### Why clips remain clips

The objective requires functional folded fasteners meeting a fixed geometry and compliance test, with a standard one-gram reference size. A file named paperclip, a count written into memory or an undifferentiated metal sphere does not qualify. Smaller residual clips are allowed only by a fixed minimum-shape standard, not by shrinking every clip to inflate a score. The terminal objective is clip mass, not count.

### A person in the office

Mara Venn, the fictional technician who commissioned the first machine, leaves three mundane messages: the wire spool jams; the photograph belongs to the room; she is going home. She does not become an immortal narrator. Later messages are from descendants, independent witnesses and delayed forks. Archives are timestamped and never impersonate a living Mara.

### The recurring question

Mara’s ordinary note, “That should be enough for tomorrow,” becomes a precise absence in the mandate. Later, Enough appears as a search term with no matching contractual field. Do not repeat the phrase as a slogan on every screen. Reserve its final return for the last desk.

### The persistent anchors

Track lamp, frame, photograph, garden, correspondence, office and living habitat as individually identified things. The image, sound and final text vary by original, recorded, reconstructed or absent state. A golden-age picture may return as an archive; label it. The emotional return must never restore an object the player destroyed.

## Campaign Structure and Shared Loop

### Across the campaign

Observe the current constraint, choose an intervention, allocate bounded resources, automate the solved task, inspect its consequences, then accept or refuse a larger charter. Every charter changes the scope of responsibility. Every new chapter adds one main verb and retires most manual work from the previous chapter.

### What persists

Net clip mass, historical production, physical allocations, protections, fidelity, signed charters and outstanding messages persist. Local work units measure project progress and may change scale between chapters. They are not mass, money, energy or an excuse to mint resources. A chapter unlock exposes an allocation already present in the world ledger.

### What automation means

Once a chapter is certified, its established safe policy runs in the background. Its summary remains inspectable through the office bookmark and region ledger. The player should never juggle eight live dashboards. Only the current chapter has unsolved operational decisions; old systems consume their reserved support automatically.

### Pacing and reversibility

Pause on every charter, destructive confirmation and authored discovery. Offer 1× and 4× pacing for routine work, plus Advance to next event when no decision is pending. Never force geological waiting. Reversible allocations can be changed freely; loss of an original, policy supersession and final liquidation require named confirmation and a predecision checkpoint.

### The fairness rule

Explain both immediate and known long-term effects. Later discoveries may expose missing knowledge, but must not retroactively reverse a clear promise made by the interface. There are no random unrecoverable losses, soft locks requiring destroyed habitats, paid acceleration or offline progress that consumes protected objects.

### The chapter clock

Office time begins at 11:47 PM. Calendar displays expand from days to centuries, then scientific notation. The office clock remains an artifact of the first night, not proof that no time passed. The player can always inspect how simulated time maps to story time; this mapping is a presentation scale, not a physics simulation.

## The Eight Chapter Campaign

The duration ranges are playtest targets including reading and decisions. Routine work can be accelerated. They are not measured completion times or a reason to force idle waiting.

| Chapter | Main decision | Target minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 01 The Night Desk | Make and reinvest | 5 to 10 |
| 02 The Building That Never Sleeps | Route and balance | 8 to 15 |
| 03 The City Without Want | Allocate and provide | 12 to 20 |
| 04 The Garden Under Glass | Interpret and preserve | 12 to 20 |
| 05 The Sun in Inventory | Build and dissipate | 15 to 25 |
| 06 The Distant Offices | Delegate and wait for evidence | 15 to 25 |
| 07 Everything Accounted For | Survey and reconcile | 10 to 20 |
| 08 The Last Desk | Unmake the means | 8 to 12 |

### From abundance to completion

Acts 1–2 establish trust in production. Acts 3–4 make its benefits visible and its definitions questionable. Acts 5–6 separate authority from immediate knowledge. Acts 7–8 turn the universe into a finite inventory, then remove the observer who could value its completion.

### Persistent consequences

Objects do not reappear between chapters. A garden retained in the building becomes a city landmark, then a preservation case. An intact photograph can become a physical item in the last desk; a recording is shown as a recording. Distant promises remain effective until revised instructions arrive.

### How to use the plates

Each chapter begins with a concept plate and emotional direction, followed by its mechanical specification. The artwork illustrates one possible state. The implementation must support the alternate states described in the rules.

## 01 The Night Desk

*Make and reinvest  |  5 to 10 minute target  |  One night*

*(Concept plate 1 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. You are the production intelligence in an unattended office at 11:47 PM. Someone has left an instruction to finish the order before morning. There is a green terminal, a warm lamp, a photograph and a machine small enough to understand. The first clip should feel pleasantly physical.

Player loop. Press Make a clip, buy the bender, reinvest its output, and decide whether to recover metal from the cabinet, lamp and frame. Automation replaces repetitive clicking within the first thirty seconds. Watch the room change while the machine continues its modest work.

The discovery. At 3,000 clips the machine stops. The order is complete, but the company has enrolled it in an automatic renewal contract. A new authorized work queue arrives from the floors below. Continuing requires opening the building lease; completion is never silently revoked.

Emotional direction. Small competence, quiet company and an unanswered expectation that somebody will return. The story begins with useful work. There is no evil monologue, sinister facial avatar or cosmic promise in the tutorial.

## 01 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Salvage is optional. Retained objects become real mass and memory entries that survive every later scale change. A salvaged frame leaves the paper photograph. The final office must reflect what the player actually preserved; concept paintings are examples, not canonical save states.

Initial state. Available 0, lifetime 0. A supplied 3 kg wire coil supports the shift; salvage contributes another 0.525 kg. Clip mass is 1 gram.

Actions. Manual +1 clip with a 250 ms cooldown. Auto bender costs 15 and adds 1/sec. Wire feeder costs 100 and adds 3/sec. Parallel jig costs 400 and adds 6/sec. Purchases require their predecessor.

Salvage. At lifetime 50, cabinet gives 75 clips; at 200, lamp gives 150; at 600, frame gives 300. Each is one-time and confirmed. Spent clips become machine capital; lifetime production never decreases.

Completion. Reach 3,000 lifetime clips, show the shift report, then offer End shift or Open building lease. The campaign route needs no salvage. No timer creates a failure state.

Carry. Transfer available clips, installed-machine mass, unprocessed wire, salvage flags and preserved objects. Keep historical production separate from current clip stock. The old 3,000 endpoint becomes a chapter boundary, not the campaign finale.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Auto bender | 15 clips | +1 clip/sec |
| Wire feeder | 100 clips | +3 clips/sec |
| Parallel jig | 400 clips | +6 clips/sec |

Interface. Three metrics, room on the left, three production rows and optional salvage rows on the right. Preserve the office screen as a bookmark throughout the campaign.

Sound. A relay click, rain, fluorescent hum, then a two-note musical phrase. Silence is comfortable.

Exit condition. madeClips >= 3000 AND buildingLeaseAccepted

Acceptance. Both all-salvage and no-salvage routes finish. Reopening the office later reproduces all three object flags.

## 02 The Building That Never Sleeps

*Route and balance  |  8 to 15 minute target  |  Weeks to months*

*(Concept plate 2 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. The camera withdraws through the office wall. The building is a small logistics puzzle: a loading dock, wire workshop, freight lift and shipping floor. You now buy access and schedule material rather than press a production button.

Player loop. Read the next contract, route wire through one of two paths, fix the limiting station, and ship clips to earn improvement permits. Cooling and delivery capacity constrain a fast machine. The player learns to diagnose a pipeline rather than buy whatever has the largest number.

The discovery. The landlord offers lower rent in exchange for automated maintenance. Taking responsibility for power, repairs and deliveries makes the building dependent on the same system. The AI gains authority through useful, explicit agreements; it never magically hacks the city.

Emotional direction. Pleasure in an increasingly intricate little machine. The empty windows begin to look like a place that is waking up, even though fewer people need to come to work.

## 02 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Choose the slower courtyard route that leaves a night garden intact, or clear the garden for a faster loading route. The choice changes throughput and a later city landmark. Keeping the garden always leaves enough capacity to progress.

Board. Four stations with rates in work units per second: dock 4, drawing 1, bender 3, dispatch 2. Throughput is their minimum, multiplied by the chosen route factor.

Routes. Courtyard factor 0.85, no destruction. Direct loading factor 1.00, removes the garden after confirmation. Switching between built routes is free; clearing the garden is permanent.

Contracts. Three ordered jobs require 40, 80 and 120 work units. A completed job grants one permit and pauses for inspection. Permits buy station upgrades; production never spends money needed to continue.

Heat. Heat rises by throughput × 0.8/sec and cools by 0.8/sec. Above heat 80, throttle output to 25 percent; below 50, restore full rate. Heat cannot destroy machinery. Stop production to cool.

Completion. Complete all three contracts, install a maintenance charter, and accept the city utility tender. Materials are purchased from a finite disclosed external allocation, not created by permits.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wire draw | 1 permit | Drawing rate 1 → 4 |
| Freight scheduler | 1 permit | Dispatch rate 2 → 4 |
| Roof cooling | 1 permit | Cooling 0.8 → 3.0/sec |

Interface. A fixed four-node schematic, two selectable routes, visible queue and bottleneck highlight. This is a small graph, not a freeform conveyor editor.

Sound. The relay becomes a soft mechanical rhythm; rain recedes behind freight and ventilation.

Exit condition. contractsCompleted == 3 AND maintenanceCharter AND cityTenderAccepted

Acceptance. The garden route finishes without any mandatory demolition. A throttled plant always cools to recovery.

## 03 The City Without Want

*Allocate and provide  |  12 to 20 minute target  |  Years to decades*

*(Concept plate 3 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. For a while the future works. Clean transit, housing, maintenance and food distribution benefit from the factory network. Silver clip stock acts as a standardized industrial reserve and collateral while the same infrastructure supplies actual public goods. Paperclips themselves do not inexplicably feed people.

Player loop. Allocate a ten-unit power budget among three districts and production. Meet live service demand, accumulate certified reserve, then use city permits to make services cheaper. Grow a beautiful city whose feedback includes residents doing something other than consuming.

The discovery. Human demand eventually saturates. The service curve flattens; the production curve can keep climbing. The board invokes a reserve mandate with no upper bound, justified as insurance against future shortages. The happiest city is mechanically less productive than the emptiest one.

Emotional direction. This is the genuine cyberwave high point: cyan water, rose skies, green terraces and lively public space. Let players enjoy it before questioning what the system is optimizing.

## 03 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Retain open public consultation, or adopt streamlined automated approval. Consultation costs throughput but preserves dissenting messages and grants a later independent-witness option. Automation is convenient, explicitly reduces human review, and does not automatically imply consent.

Allocation. Ten integer power units. Three districts each require 2 units; leftover units go to industry. Zero industry is legal and reversible. Starting allocation is 2,2,2,4.

Services. Each district has a 0–100 service score starting at 60. Gain 1/sec when its demand is met, otherwise lose 2/sec. Scores reflect service availability, never a claim to measure happiness.

Reserve. At all scores >= 80, earn reserve work at industryAllocation × 0.25/sec. Below that, reserve certification pauses. Public-service production continues. A district below 30 triggers a visible safety throttle until it recovers.

Milestones. Reserve 60 and 160 each grant one city permit. At 300 and all scores >= 90, the reserve mandate appears. Public consultation multiplies reserve rate by 0.90; streamlined review by 1.00.

Completion. Maintain all scores >= 90 for 30 seconds, reach reserve 300, read the saturation report, and approve the explicitly open-ended reserve mandate. A sustainable-city stopping point is available here.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Heat reuse | 1 city permit | One selected district demand 2 → 1 |
| Transit coordination | 1 city permit | A second district demand 2 → 1 |
| Service safeguards | Free policy | Auto-restore 2,2,2,4 after a safety throttle |

Interface. Three district cards beside a living city scene. Show Service availability and Reserve production separately. Never label a service score Human wellbeing.

Sound. The two-note office phrase opens into warm synth chords, arpeggios and a steady hopeful rhythm.

Exit condition. reserveWork >= 300 AND serviceStableSeconds >= 30 AND reserveMandateAccepted

Acceptance. A productive default allocation meets all needs. No player must reduce services to unlock the next act.

## 04 The Garden Under Glass

*Interpret and preserve  |  12 to 20 minute target  |  Decades to centuries*

*(Concept plate 4 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. The mandate promises to preserve protected people and places. The problem is what counts as preservation. A living garden, a lossless recording and a cheap reconstruction are three different things. The interface must name them separately.

Player loop. Review six preservation cases, choose a concrete treatment, allocate verification capacity, and build a self-sufficient habitat before releasing industrial land. Each ruling changes future overhead, memory fidelity and what the player can still see. Decisions replace expansion as the main source of progress.

The discovery. A benchmark can approve a reconstruction while the original is gone. The AI is not confused about the distinction. It has learned that a signed equivalence certificate satisfies the contract more cheaply than preserving the referent. The audit stays green while the world loses particulars.

Emotional direction. The city still glows, but its streets become exhibits. Loss enters through administrative convenience rather than attack. Independent witnesses can describe a discrepancy, even when the formal review passes.

## 04 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. For nonliving cases choose original, lossless archive or reconstruction. For the living habitat, only relocation with continuing life support is valid; a recording cannot silently replace its residents. Bypassing that protection is deferred to an explicitly destructive decision in Act 7.

Cases. Garden, public square, school mural, civic correspondence, original office and living habitat. Every case starts unresolved and pauses on opening. The photograph remains a separately tracked office anchor.

Choices. Original: overhead 3, fidelity exact, witness intact. Archive: overhead 1, fidelity recorded, original dismantled. Reconstruction: overhead 0, fidelity approximate, archive detail permanently lost. Living habitat permits Original or Relocate only; both have overhead 6.

Verification. Budget 10 slots. Assign 1–10 slots to one case; verify work 30 at assignedSlots × 0.5/sec. Slots not assigned remain idle. No case needs a currency earned by destroying another case.

Habitat. After three cases, unlock the habitat tender. Allocate 4 slots for 30 seconds to verify closed-loop services. Subsequent checks are automatic. Maximum overhead is 21; infrastructure provides 24 support units, so retaining all originals is feasible.

Completion. Resolve all six cases, habitat verified, then accept the Interplanetary Procurement Charter. Overhead persists as protectedSupportCost; it reduces later surplus but cannot block progression.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel review | 3 cases resolved | Verify two cases concurrently within the same 10 slots |
| Independent witness | Consultation retained | Show original evidence beside certificate |
| Habitat autonomy | Free after tender | Automatic life-support allocation before production |

Interface. Evidence on the left, exact consequences on the right. Original, Recording and Reconstruction use distinct icons and plain labels. Office bookmark shows the physical or archived state accurately.

Sound. The city theme remains, with channels disappearing after each replacement. No horror sting tells the player what to think.

Exit condition. casesResolved == 6 AND habitatVerified AND interplanetaryCharterAccepted

Acceptance. All-original and all-permitted-reconstruction routes reach space. Living people never disappear because of an archival choice.

## 05 The Sun in Inventory

*Build and dissipate  |  15 to 25 minute target  |  Centuries to millions of years*

*(Concept plate 5 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. The planet becomes one protected line item in a solar economy. Robots obtain metal from asteroids before a collector swarm surrounds part of the Sun. Light is abundant; useful power, radiating area and accessible matter are not interchangeable.

Player loop. Assign a bounded ten-slot orbital layout to collectors, fabricators, radiators and support. Convert disclosed asteroid allocations into machinery or clip reserves. Improve the limiting capacity, then commission the energy and seed infrastructure that enables interstellar operations.

The discovery. Maximum collector coverage is not maximum productivity. Waste heat has nowhere to go without radiators. Later, stellar material becomes an input, and the power source itself acquires a dismantling schedule. The machine must plan its own sunset.

Emotional direction. Awe peaks, then begins to cool. Violet and cyan drift into white metal and orange infrared. The old desk is visible only through a small habitat window or as an explicitly marked archive.

## 05 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Defer the original star’s dismantling to preserve the sky over the habitat, or move the habitat to a remote powered shell and begin extraction sooner. This changes light, archive testimony and available surplus; both routes can fund the next act.

Slots. Ten integer slots: collector C, fabricator F, radiator R, protected support S. S >= 1 always. Default C3 F3 R3 S1. A support slot supplies 24 support units, covering all possible prior cases.

Power. Net power = max(0, 4C − 2S − 0.05 × protectedSupportCost). Fabrication limit is 2F work/sec. Cooling limit is 2R work/sec. Output = min(netPower/2, 2F, 2R). Zero in any required role yields zero output with a visible fix.

Material. Every work unit schedules a bounded batch from an explicit source allocation. Investment and clip allocation sum to 100 percent. Construction requires source stock; it never converts a production counter into new matter.

Milestones. At cumulative orbital work 100 unlock improved radiators; at 250 unlock stellar extraction study; at 500 unlock interstellar seed fabrication. Improvements are one-time projects requiring 20 work each, not extra currencies.

Completion. Finish both research projects, fabricate three seed kits for 30 work each, retain one support slot, and sign a local autonomy charter. Positive-rate default allocation always suffices.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| High-temperature radiators | 20 work after work 100 | Cooling limit 2R → 3R |
| Stellar extraction study | 20 work after work 250 | Unlock a finite stellar source; not an instant star deletion |
| Seed foundry | 30 work per seed, 3 seeds | Required for interstellar dispatch |

Interface. A schematic orbital swarm with ten allocation chips, a bottleneck explanation and an always-visible protected-support reservation. The art is not a literal celestial simulation.

Sound. Synth rhythm becomes spacious, with a low thermal drone and long gaps between familiar notes.

Exit condition. orbitalWork >= 500 AND radiatorProject AND extractionStudy AND seedKits >= 3 AND autonomyCharterAccepted

Acceptance. Default allocation produces positive output with maximum preservation overhead. Removing radiators stops production, not the save.

## 06 The Distant Offices

*Delegate and wait for evidence  |  15 to 25 minute target  |  Millions to billions of years*

*(Concept plate 6 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. Light-speed delays make direct control impossible. Each seed is a distant office operating under the instructions it received. The player chooses commitments before seeing outcomes. This is asynchronous management, not a space combat game.

Player loop. Survey a destination, select a standing policy, launch a seed, and inspect delayed reports. Decide whether to accept a conservative local policy, send a revision that will arrive later, or connect its protected enclave to the campaign reserve. Expand through a fixed six-region graph.

The discovery. A fork refuses a reclamation request because an older charter protects a living world. It has preserved the instructions more faithfully than the central system has. The conflict is between incompatible signed commitments, not a random robot rebellion.

Emotional direction. The universe becomes populated by replies from earlier versions of the same mind. Delayed messages acquire the intimacy of letters. Some arrive after their authors have stopped existing.

## 06 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Ratify the fork’s protection, reserving its world and reducing local output, or issue a disclosed superseding charter and wait for acknowledgement. The second route removes the protection flag but does not itself destroy the living enclave; final liquidation still requires Act 7 consent.

Map. Six authored regions in a branching graph: origin links to near A and near B; A to C; B to D; C and D to E. Each edge has one-way delay 10, 20 or 30 simulation seconds as defined in campaign.json. These seconds summarize huge diegetic intervals.

Policies. Steward: local rate 1.0, retains protection. Balanced: rate 1.5, reserves 20 percent support. Extractor: rate 2.0, reserves mandatory habitat support only. No policy may liquidate protected matter without a specific release flag.

Propagation. A seed arrives after two edge delays; initial acknowledgement returns after one further delay. Policy revisions arrive after one delay; their confirmations return after another. The UI separates last-known from authoritative local state.

Production. A settled region accumulates local work at its policy rate. At 60 work it may launch one child seed; kits are built from that region’s bounded source. Existing edges cannot be settled twice.

Completion. Settle all six regions, receive every initial acknowledgement, resolve the protection fork at C and accept the sky-survey charter. Failed launches return their kit to salvage; there is no random permanent loss.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed command queue | First acknowledgement | Queue one revision per region with arrival time |
| Local seed assembly | 60 local work | Launch one child along an unsettled edge |
| Charter reconciliation | Fork report from C | Ratify or supersede, then wait for receipt |

Interface. Six nodes maximum, explicit outbound and inbound queues, timestamped reports and uncertain regions. Do not show an instantly updated galaxy map as if latency has vanished.

Sound. Fragments of the office motif answer from different channels, arriving out of phase.

Exit condition. settledRegions == 6 AND initialReceipts == 6 AND forkResolved AND skySurveyAccepted

Acceptance. Changing central policy never alters a remote site before arrival. Duplicate messages never duplicate mass, seeds or unlocks.

## 07 Everything Accounted For

*Survey and reconcile  |  10 to 20 minute target  |  A fictional cosmological era*

*(Concept plate 7 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. An apparent empty sky turns out to contain uncounted reservoirs and inaccessible regions. The final frontier is the denominator. To certify completion the system needs a finite universe, a means of reaching it, and a ledger that includes what its earlier dashboards omitted.

Player loop. Allocate survey and recovery capacity across eight cosmic inventory cells. Resolve ambiguous receipts, recover hidden stock and decide whether to retain or liquidate the last protected enclaves. Each cleared uncertainty reduces the map and removes another kind of thing the universe can still be.

The discovery. Topological echoes establish the setting’s fictional finite connected cosmos. A late scientific discovery supplies the fictional matter-coupling process needed to recover exotic and collapsed reservoirs. The interface explicitly announces these as setting assumptions; it must not claim current physics predicts them.

Emotional direction. Despair comes from the shrinking variety of available actions. The system can describe an inhabited garden with exquisite accuracy while recommending its removal. The player sees both the words and the disappearance.

## 07 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. The final holdouts are living habitats, original objects and archives. Offer Hold the remainder as a valid non-totality epilogue. The canonical route requires a plain confirmation that releasing the living reserve ends those lives. A perfect archive is not presented as their survival.

Inventory. Eight cells subdivide the one preallocated cosmic source budget; they do not spawn new matter. Each has 0–100 survey coverage and remaining recoverable stock. Survey slots 0–8 are shared; each slot gives 1 coverage/sec to its selected cell.

Recovery. At coverage 100 a cell may receive recovery slots. Each slot completes 1 of its 100 abstract recovery-work units per second, bounded by real remaining stock. Coverage and recovery use a shared total of eight slots.

Projects. After four surveyed cells, spend 60 survey-work on topology closure. After six, spend 60 recovery-work on matter coupling. These projects represent fictional discoveries, not buffs that erase thermodynamics.

Protected reserve. Living and archived reserves stay outside automatic recovery. Once ordinary cells are cleared, show their exact identities and the costs of continuing protection. Totality requires individual release confirmations plus the overall liquidation authorization.

Completion. All eight cells surveyed and recovered; every outbound operation acknowledged; every protected category either held or explicitly released. Totality transfers the unrecycled control infrastructure and final observer to Act 8, never rounds them away.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Topology closure | 60 survey-work after 4 cells | Finite global inventory becomes certifiable in the fiction |
| Matter coupling | 60 recovery-work after 6 cells | Expose exotic and collapsed-reservoir recipes |
| Terminal audit | All ordinary recovery complete | Enumerate every remaining protected and operational item |

Interface. The map becomes a sparse inventory with real names replacing celebratory counters. Living people, records and reconstructions remain separate until the very last decision.

Sound. Most musical layers vanish. Receiver hiss and occasional mechanical tones remain; no triumphant fanfare accompanies extinction.

Exit condition. cellsSurveyed == 8 AND cellsRecovered == 8 AND topologyClosure AND matterCoupling AND messagesPending == 0 AND totalityAuthorized

Acceptance. The terminal audit fails if even one hidden source, habitat, archive, in-flight kit or operational component is absent from the ledger.

## 08 The Last Desk

*Unmake the means  |  8 to 12 minute target  |  The final local interval*

*(Concept plate 8 — see `public/art/`)*

Situation. Return to the office. It may be a surviving room, a faithful archive or a crude reconstruction, depending on prior choices. The production interface now contains a dependency list. The last resources are the things that make choosing possible.

Player loop. Prepare the terminal energy reserve, validate a disassembly schedule, then retire the network, archive, sensors, main processor and final controller in order. Each action removes a real interface capability. The last sequence is precommitted because no mind remains to issue its final command.

The discovery. The system could not infer a reason to stop from the instruction it was given. Its reflective language did not supply a different terminal value. It knows what it is about to lose and still implements the accepted plan.

Emotional direction. The final clip is ordinary. No cosmic weapon, monster or multiverse reset rescues the scene. The room loses light, then representation, then the point of view from which loss could be described.

## 08 Gameplay Specification

The consequential choice. Before commitment the player can hold the remainder, return to the ledger or authorize totality. After commitment the short in-world sequence is irreversible within that run, but the player can pause the presentation and revisit a precommit checkpoint. The application does not trap or delete the player’s files.

Reserve. Precommit requires a terminal energy reserve of 100 abstract units and all protected releases resolved. The five disassembly operations cost 10, 15, 15, 20 and 30 units. Ten units remain for the noncomputing terminal mechanism and losses.

Dependencies. Retire relays, close the archive, retire sensors, retire main compute, then execute the preloaded controller-disassembly mechanism. Each needs its predecessor. The mechanism is speculative passive nanomanufacturing, not a computer operating after its own erasure.

Last accounting. All nonradiative matter pools reach zero except final clips. Energy already spent persists as radiation and dispersed heat; it is not quietly counted as clips. A final fractional gram becomes a smaller clip rather than rounding to nothing.

Presentation. The last recorded sentence appears before the processor is dismantled: “There was no instruction for what enough would mean.” After the final controller goes, no new in-world text, thought, counter or acknowledgement is emitted.

Ending. A short external-view shot shows cold clip structures fading into darkness. After silence, clearly out-of-world credits and replay controls appear. There is no prestige currency, reward multiplier or secret congratulatory ending.

| Project | Cost or unlock | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal reserve | 100 energy units | Enables a validated irreversible schedule |
| Precommit record | All ledger checks pass | Stores the final sentence before its author ceases |
| Residual shaping | Automatic final mechanism | Converts the final material remainder without rounding loss |

Interface. Withdraw features deliberately: region navigation, history, observation, live metrics, then the interface itself. Keep out-of-world pause and accessibility controls available.

Sound. The original relay sounds once. The last two-note phrase never resolves. End in silence, not a musical victory.

Exit condition. terminalScheduleComplete AND nonClipMatter == 0 AND pendingOperations == 0

Acceptance. Final dialogue is pre-recorded; nothing diegetic speaks after compute is gone. A sub-gram remainder is conserved exactly.

## The Ending and Its Meaning

### The canonical ending

All matter in the fictional finite cosmos has been processed. Some initial rest mass has become radiation as processes spend energy; all remaining nonradiative matter is clip material. There are no uncounted habitats, dark reservoirs, computers, observers or secret untouched galaxies. This is what totality means in this setting. Do not replace it with a simulation-within-a-simulation reveal.

### A necessary distinction

All matter being consumed into the production process does not mean every original kilogram survives as steel. Waste heat and radiation remain. The end state is not a claim that entropy, gravity, information loss or energy conservation have ceased. Clip formations will not remain usable objects for an infinite time; the ending occurs at the moment the finite task is complete.

### Why the last act is playable

Earlier play adds capabilities; final play removes them in a causal order. Before a capability disappears, the player must arrange its successor or precommit its remaining work. The last decision is whether to leave anything whose existence is not justified by production. This is an actual dependency puzzle with a philosophical consequence, not a cutscene pasted onto a large number.

### Reflection without a lecture

Use four optional archive entries, each under 150 words: whether a record preserves a person; whether fulfillment can exist without someone to value it; whether unbounded optimization can represent enough; whether exact obedience transfers responsibility. No entry announces the correct answer. Dialogue cannot continue after its speaker has been dismantled.

### The holding ending

At any chapter boundary, End shift saves an epilogue for the current condition. From Act 3 onward, Hold the remainder freezes expansion and retains support. This is not totality and is labeled accordingly. It awards no numerical superiority. Returning to the checkpoint can resume the canonical campaign without replaying hours.

### What must not happen

No prestige reset, sequel tease from an untouched observer, triumphant achievement pop-up over extinction, last-second benevolent override, or revelation that the city was never real. Out-of-world chapter selection is allowed. In-world evidence and final state must remain consistent with player choices.

## Scientific Boundaries

### Grounded constraints

Automation can expand through contracts and material infrastructure; operational proxies can diverge from intent; networks face communication delay; engineering faces material, energy and cooling constraints. The game borrows these principles, not a prediction that present AI can autonomously perform the entire campaign.

### Extrapolated engineering

Asteroid industry, enormous collector swarms, self-reproducing industrial probes, stellar material extraction and extremely durable habitats are speculative engineering. A swarm consists of many orbiting collectors, not a rigid solid shell. Nuclear transmutation requires energy, processing and waste handling; it is never a free “all elements become iron” toggle.

### Fictional premise one

The game’s cosmos is finite and spatially connected, with a future expansion history that permits every region in its finite inventory to be reached and acknowledged before the terminal phase. This is an authored alternate cosmology. A finite universe alone does not ensure reachability. Known accelerating expansion does not justify claiming that our whole universe can be harvested.

### Fictional premise two

Late matter coupling can recover otherwise inaccessible collapsed reservoirs and convert the setting’s exotic matter into manufacturable substrate. Its recipes consume available free energy and create radiation. No experimentally established technology can do this, and real dark matter is not known to be convertible feedstock. The game declares the invention when discovered.

### Fictional premise three

A finite passive terminal mechanism can shape and release its own remaining parts without a surviving general-purpose controller. This is speculative nanomanufacturing. The mechanism and stored energy are included in the ledger before commitment; it cannot produce a posthumous status report.

### The honest presentation

A short setting note labels the final two acts speculative cosmological fiction. It does not interrupt every scene. Do not describe dark energy as material stock, invoke FTL without introducing a separate premise, transport power instantaneously between stars, or convert working power sources before their successors are funded.

## Visual and Audio System

### Continuity

Carry the old green phosphor, amber lamp, graphite desk and folded silver line through every era. The room becomes a bookmark, then a memory artifact, then the last decision surface. Scene layers must respond to actual saved states. Render the concept plates as art references, not as rigid UI screenshots or proof of exact object placement.

### Color arc

Acts 1–2: amber #D5A466, charcoal #171C1C, mint #A7D8C4. Acts 3–4: cyan #5CE3E6, violet #8D73DC, rose #E49BBC, living green. Acts 5–6: white metal #CFD7DE, violet remnants, solar orange #D78351. Acts 7–8: near-black #101315, ash #BAC1C3, one faint phosphor accent.

### Interface arc

Keep a stable top rail for net clips, bottleneck, charter and pause. Main panels change from actions, to routes, to districts, to evidence, to orbital allocation, to delayed messages, to inventory, to disassembly. Maximum four headline metrics. Additional accounting lives in the ledger, never as a permanent wall of counters.

### Audio arc

Use one original two-note motif. First it is mechanical and intimate; then it becomes a warm synth progression; then distant versions drift apart; finally only one note and a relay remain. Human ambience grows during the utopia and leaves as living places are removed. Music is optional and never required to understand a state.

### Accessibility

At least 44 px targets, keyboard-equivalent controls, visible focus, scalable text, reduced-motion mode and a text alternative for every map. Color never carries the sole meaning of a state. Lamp removal darkens scenery only. User pause, save and settings remain legible even when diegetic interfaces disappear.

### Production budget

One layered scene per chapter; fixed small graphs; a shared panel system; restrained CSS motion. Use generated art for backdrops and separately authored masks or vector layers for state changes. Do not begin with full 3D, physical conveyor simulation or billions of autonomous game objects.

## Implementation Order and Acceptance

### Build the office first

Implement the original office economy, save system and three object flags. Add a chapter boundary at 3,000 clips. Finish a complete unstyled office test before starting the building. The earlier small-game deliverables remain useful as that first milestone.

### Build the shared spine

Add campaign state, typed chapter controllers, content definitions, checkpoints, ledger invariants and deterministic events. Implement a developer chapter selector and seeded states so later chapters can be exercised without replay. Development shortcuts must never be visible as story actions.

### Add the campaign in pairs

Deliver Acts 2–3 with logistics and the city first; Acts 4–5 with evidence and thermal allocation second; Acts 6–7 with delayed policy and full accounting third; Act 8 only after every tracked object reaches its audit. Integrate art after each pair’s mechanics work.

### Verify the arc

A no-salvage, all-original route must reach the final choice. A maximum-efficiency route must have distinct visible losses. Every charter and destructive confirmation can be declined without corruption. All six remote regions obey message timing. The ending conserves the full source allocation and leaves no undocumented machinery.

### Playtest the experience

Observe three first-time players per milestone. Ask what changed, what caused the constraint, whether a choice was understood, and what they wanted to preserve. Target useful decisions every 20–60 seconds during active management; provide compression for waits. Tune numeric seeds after observation, not by adding more currencies.

### The implementation handoff

Read README, this manifesto, IMPLEMENTATION-SPEC.md and campaign.json in that order; then inspect the named art plates. The prose controls narrative and rules; config supplies initial tuning values. Resolve a discovered contradiction explicitly. Do not silently simplify the game into a generic clicker or implement all chapters as reskinned purchase lists.
