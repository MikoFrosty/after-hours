# Implementation notes

This file records how the implementation reads the manifesto, `IMPLEMENTATION-SPEC.md`, `campaign.json` and `NARRATIVE.md`, and what was decided where they were silent. Nothing here changes a stated rule; where a rule needed an interpretation, the interpretation is named so it can be revisited after playtesting.

## Authority order

Manifesto (fiction, arc, meaning) → implementation spec (simulation, accounting, edge cases) → `campaign.json` (numbers) → narrative (exact copy). Concept plates are used as backdrops; state that must be accurate (office objects, the garden, fidelity) is drawn as SVG layers from the save, never inferred from a painting.

## Ledger interpretation

- **Account categories** are exactly the eight in the spec. Each account also carries a `kind` so the terminal audit can enumerate *originals*, *records*, *reconstructions* and *living reserves* separately.
- **Protected originals** (cabinet, lamp, frame, photograph, garden, square, mural, correspondence) live in `archiveProtected` with `kind: original`. The category name is read as "preservation-protected material", covering both retained originals and archive storage.
- **Office equipment** (desk, terminal, machine, room fixtures) is `capital`. Choosing *Original* for the office case in Act 4 marks it protected; archiving it moves 1/10 to archive storage and the rest to raw.
- **Authored masses** not given by the spec (all fictional): night garden 2 t; public square 5 kt; school mural 3 t; civic correspondence 500 kg; living habitat 10¹⁸ µg; the world below region C 6 × 10³³ µg; each Act 5 construction project 10²⁷ µg. They are carved out of the chapter's disclosed grant, never added to it.
- **Lifetime production** counts every clip formed, including salvage yields (the office economy counts salvage toward the 3,000 order). If salvage would exceed the order, only the remaining quota forms clips; the rest is raw scrap.
- **Transactions** are atomic and validated (input exists, protection permits it, outputs including radiation equal input). Discrete transactions are de-duplicated by ID; per-step production batches are deterministic within the step and are not stored individually.

## Chapter decisions

**01. The office night (rebuilt at the owner's request).** The chapter is now a complete small game of 20+ minutes, replacing the spec's 5–10 minute office. Deliberate departures from the spec, approved by the project owner:

- *Plain words throughout* (owner's request: "bench" and "die" meant nothing to most players). The player-facing names are now: the **clip machine** (was the bench bender), **machine oil**, the **wire spring** (tensioner), the **wire feeder**, **fast / gentle bending** (the high-tension / smooth die), the **second bending arm** (second die), the **auto-packer**, the **wire guide** (guide roller), the **foot pedal** and **auto-boost** (governor), the **desk fan**, the **side-table rack** with **small machines 1–6** (jig frame and stations), the **faster / careful line** (overdrive / careful finish) and the **wire recycler** (straightener). The wire **snags** (was "catches"); hand presses after the feeder **boost the machines** (was "tend the line"); true wire is a **speed burst**; tuning the die is **fine-tuning the machines**; line speeds are **Normal / Fast / Very fast**; ruined clips are **spoiled**. Internal ids are unchanged so saves load.
- *Systems arrive one at a time.* Speed bursts start with the wire feeder (not with the first machine), and fine-tuning opens with the wire guide (after the packer), so the first minutes are just: make clips, buy the machine, oil it, free the snags.

- *The order counts sealed cartons, not lifetime clips.* 3,000 clips = 12 cartons of 250. Clips spent on machinery must be bent again, so every purchase is a trade between going faster later and finishing sooner. Lifetime production can exceed 3,000.
- *A 3 kg spare coil* sits in the cabinet's bottom drawer (disclosed by the facilities inventory, or offered automatically when wire runs low). Total wire 6 kg covers the order, every machine and the ruined clips. Taking the coil does not touch the cabinet.
- *Hand bending has no speed limit* (the 250 ms cooldown is gone). Once the wire feeder is installed, the feeder owns the wire and hand presses *tend the line* instead of forming clips, so clicking speed can never outrun the order.
- *Installations replace the three upgrades*, revealed one at a time, each paid from clips on the desk and taking a few seconds to install: calibrate, oil the die, spring tensioner, wire feeder, second die, auto-packer, guide roller, desk fan, a jig frame and six jig stations, and a wire straightener. The jig is staged so the late night still has purchases in it: stations 4, 5 and 6 each wait for more sealed cartons (5, 7, 9), and the next-goal line names that wait. Numbers live in `campaign.json` (`office.projects`).
- *Three either/or choices* (one or the other, for the rest of the night):
  - **The die** (after the feeder): a *high-tension die* (bender ×1.35, but the wire catches again every 300 machine clips) or a *smooth die* (×1.15, never catches, tending drains half as fast).
  - **The hands** (after the guide roller): a *foot pedal* (tending can add up to +40% instead of +25%) or a *governor* (the meter never drains below half). The pedal rewards effort, the governor forgives absence; both keep the main button.
  - **The finish** (after the third station): *overdrive* (the whole line ×1.2, about 1 in 25 more clips ruined at every speed) or a *careful finish* (nothing ruined at any speed; clean runs last 30 s).
- *The wire catches* in the bender's guide every 25 machine clips (60 with the tensioner) until the feeder is installed; the line stops until the player frees it. This is Mara's first note made playable. After the feeder it catches again only with the high-tension die, or every 200 clips at *hard* speed until a straightener is installed; the two never stack.
- *Line speed* (after the jig frame): steady, brisk ×1.25 with ~5% ruined, hard ×1.45 with ~12% ruined. Ruined clips go to a rejects account, never lost; the straightener draws them back into wire.
- *The auto-packer saves for you*: it always leaves the price of the next upgrade on the desk and packs everything above it; once nothing is left to buy it packs every clip. (The previous version had a reserve setting the player had to manage; it was a frequent trap, with clips packed away that were needed for the next machine.)
- *Sealing is always the player's*: a full carton waits on the packer until it is taped shut (the dock's slot, the Seal button or S). Twelve small rituals across the night, with the van giving them urgency.
- *A soft deadline*: a delivery van stops across the street at 5:22 AM and takes whatever is sealed; the rest go on the 7:00 run. Nothing fails. The van's log line and the shift report say how many cartons made it.
- *No fast-forward in the office.* At the owner's request, 1×/4× and Next event are absent for the whole chapter; the line is sped up by playing it instead:
  - **Tending**: each hand press fills a meter (5% a press) that drains at 4% a second; a full meter glows and adds up to +25% (+40% with the pedal) to machine output, and while it is over half full the next true-wire moment comes round half again as fast. **Practice** (every 150 presses, three levels) makes each press tend half again as much.
  - **Speed bursts** (true wire): once the wire feeder is in, the wire runs perfectly straight every 50–90 s. Catching it within 10 s (button, C key, or clicking the glint on the spool) gives a 20 s clean run at ×1.6 and frees a caught wire. Missing one costs nothing.
  - **Fine-tuning** (after the wire guide): stop a swinging needle inside a band for +4% a level (five levels; the band narrows each time). A new setting has to run in for 25 s before the next attempt; a miss costs a 6 s wait. *Tune slowly* always succeeds in 20 s, for anyone who prefers not to play the timing game. Levels are permanent: the die wear of the previous version was removed (it took progress away and was the least liked part of the night); the cost of running fast is spoiled clips and snags.
  Later chapters keep their 1×/4× pacing for now.
- *Story time runs at a steady pace* (about 17 story seconds per second), so the 5:22 van comes a little over nineteen minutes in for everyone, and holds just short of dawn until the last carton; then the sky catches up over a few seconds. Outside the window the floors go dark, a tram passes, the rain eases, the van stops, the sky turns grey; each is also told in the caption strip. Cartons that left on the van are gone from the stack once it pulls away. Birds start once the rain thins.
- *Terminal files* (the order, the contract with its §9 continuation clause that activates on completion, the bender manual, the facilities inventory, a lamp ticket, a building memo that sets up chapter 2). Mara still leaves only her three messages (the photograph note now arrives when the jig frame goes in beside it); after the first clip her latest note collapses to one line that opens on request.
- *The desk is a fixed action dock* at the top of the panel (it stays in view while the rest scrolls): the main button with floating +1 / +% feedback, the tending meter, one fixed-height slot for whatever needs attention (caught wire, true wire, clean run, installation, otherwise the next goal, which also names the next clip-count unlock from the very first clip), the twelve cartons with the van status and the pack/tape button, and a line saying what the packer is keeping back. The rail's fourth item shows what the night is waiting on ("Saving for a die", "Wire caught", "Line at full speed") instead of a rate.
- *Feedback in the room*: clips the machines add float up off the tray once a second, sealed cartons drop onto the stack, jig stations appear in their sockets, and a progress ring sits on whatever is being installed. Story captions sit in a strip at the top of the scene, clear of the desk.
- *Sound*: single clacks per clip at a slow rate thin out into a soft running texture as the line speeds up; the jig strikes in an uneven long-short-short. A low pad comes in under the rain after two in the morning and opens toward dawn. The two-note motif plays only at the night's turning points (first clip, feeder, jig frame, last carton). New cues: a soft ping the first time each installation becomes affordable, a full carton waiting, a carton landing on the stack, the van, and a swell when the sixth station goes in.
- The shift report waits six seconds after the last carton so the room can go quiet first.

- *No salvage in the night* (owner's decision): the night is about slow growth, so the cabinet, lamp and frame are not offered for metal here. They are offered at the start of chapter 2 instead, when the 11th floor is cleared for the lease (see 02).

Measured with the simulated players in `autopilot.ts` (relaxed: smooth die, governor, careful finish; engaged: high-tension die, pedal, overdrive): a relaxed player (tends in short bursts, notices true wire and full cartons after a few seconds, catches two in three glints, hits half its tunings) takes about 21 minutes and gets nine cartons on the 5:22 van; an engaged player who plays every mechanic hard takes about 16 and gets all twelve. Purchases continue until about 19 minutes for the relaxed player; the longest stretch without a purchase, carton, file or offer is about 90 s, while carton two is being filled for the jig frame (the tram passes in the middle of it). After the plain-language pass the numbers are about 22 and 17 minutes. Tests guard both bounds. Older saves in the office are migrated in place (the packer's old share becomes a reserve; a jig bought whole becomes a frame with four stations; a pending office salvage decision is dropped).

**02. The building (rebuilt at the owner's request, third version).** 15–20 minutes, built around one rule (*the line runs as fast as its slowest room*) and introducing one thing per contract. The second version (queues, permits spent in inspections) put too much on screen at once; this one starts with a single button.

- *Clips are the currency.* Every clip the building makes counts toward the current contract and is added to *clips to spend*. Purchases cost clips, with prices that grow geometrically per room (`building.rooms` in `campaign.json`).
- *Four rooms, in order*: the **loading dock** (trucks bring coils of wire), the **wire room** (thins the supplier's thick rod into wire), the **workshop** (the clip machines from the 11th floor) and **shipping** (the freight lift to the trucks). Rooms not yet open are drawn as dashed boxes. The dock fills a wire buffer; the rooms after it turn that wire into clips at the rate of the slowest of them.
- *One new thing per contract.* (1) **First load**, 200 clips: the dock has no one on it; the only action is *Unload a coil of wire* (button, D or B, or clicking the dock), 5 clips of wire a coil, two coils a second at most. (2) **Room to grow**, 700: the shop opens (hire a dock hand, add a clip machine); the slowest room is highlighted, including the dock while no one is hired. (3) **Rod, not wire**, 1,800: the wire room. Rush orders start (a phone call: make N clips in 45 s for a bonus; nothing is lost if you miss one). (4) **Across town**, 3,500: shipping. (5) **The long run**, 6,000: workshop heat (fans, throttle at 80 back to 50 at quarter speed, no heat added while throttled, so it always recovers; a manual stop button). (6) **The courtyard**, 9,500: roadworks send the trucks round the night garden (dock at 85%); paving it is offered, never required.
- *Clearing the 11th floor* (the office's salvage, moved here from chapter 1) is asked once, after the first delivery: bring everything down as it is, or decide one by one. Salvaged items add clips to spend; the photograph is always kept.
- *No fast-forward in chapters 1 and 2.* Story time runs at six story hours a second.

Measured with the simulated players: a relaxed player takes about 17–18 minutes; an engaged one about 14. Tests guard 15–20 and ≥10. Older chapter-2 saves (both previous versions) are converted to the room levels they imply.

**Demo end.** The demo stops after chapter 2 on an *End of the demo* screen (the night's and the building's play time, what happened to the old office and the garden, rush orders won) with *Play again* and *Back to the title*. `demo.endsAfter` in `campaign.json` sets the chapter; simulated players and tests set a flag to play the full campaign.

**Debug tools.** The backquote key (`) or the *Debug* button opens a panel: game speed 1/2/5/10/30×, +100 / +1,000 / +10,000 clips, finish the current order or contract, restart the night or jump to the building, and continue past the demo end.

**03.** Allocation moves one unit at a time between a district and industry (industry is the donor for `+`, the recipient for `−`). The consultation decision is asked on the first full service. Resident vignettes appear only while every district is at 80+, as feedback that is not consumption. The mandate appears only after 30 s of stable service at 90+ and reserve 300.

**04.** A case's treatment is chosen, then *committed to verification* (with the preservation certificate for archive/reconstruction/relocation). Opening a case for the first time pauses play so its evidence can be read. After three cases the habitat tender opens and parallel review allows two cases at once within the same ten slots. The spec's "decisions replace expansion" is honoured with a small background surplus whose rate is `(24 − overhead) × 0.1` work/s, so protected support visibly narrows later output without ever blocking progress. The independent witness (earned by keeping public consultation) shows original evidence beside every certificate and contributes a letter.

**05.** The star decision is asked when the extraction study completes. *Defer* keeps the sky and applies ×0.90. *Relocate* applies ×0.90 until the 30-work relocation moves the habitat account, with its living and protected flags, to a powered shell; the *sky* anchor then becomes absent.

**06.** Orders to remote regions are messages: they arrive after the region's shortest-path delay and execute there (survey 10 s → assemble a kit from 60 local work if none is in stock → launch with the policy frozen into the seed). Only messages the central office itself sent are drawn in flight; inbound evidence is invisible until it arrives. The spec's "deterministic authored reason" for a failed child is used once: the first seed toward region B loses guidance, stays on the ledger in transit, and can be recovered for 10 origin work with a return delay. The sky-survey charter is offered only when no kit is adrift, so every transit kit belongs to exactly one account before Act 7. *Ratify* halves region C's local rate. *Supersede* resolves only when the amendment's receipt returns; the living account stays protected until Act 7's explicit release.

**07.** Survey slots move to recovery automatically when a cell completes (or are freed when an exotic cell still needs matter coupling). The terminal audit is a query over every ledger account with non-clip mass. Authorization assembles the 1,000 kg terminal machine first from the retained office items (so the last desk is literally the machine that unmakes itself), then from other capital, then from raw, and only then sweeps everything else to clips.

**08.** The five tasks run at 0, 8, 18, 28 and 38 s of a 45 s precommitted sequence. Captions appear only before main compute retires; the final sentence is shown from 20 s to its retirement at 28 s; afterwards there is no new in-world text. The exterior shot uses a crop of plate 7 without the terrarium (which would contradict a liquidated living reserve), then silence, then credits with the ordinary out-of-world actions and no grade.

## Presentation choices

- The rail shows exactly four headline items: clips on hand, one chapter metric, bottleneck, charter; plus the story clock and pace controls.
- Story time is a presentation scale (hover the clock to inspect it): about 17 story seconds per second in the office (dawn waits for the last carton), 6 hours in the building, 20 days in the city, a year in preservation, 5,000 years in the solar era, 10⁷ years across distant offices, 10¹⁰ years in the cosmic inventory.
- Act 4 art desaturates a little with every non-original outcome; Act 7 art darkens as cells are cleared. Act 4 music loses one channel per replaced original.
- Office variants: *archived* renders as a phosphor wireframe recording; *reconstructed* drops small details (notes, calendar, rain, the photograph's image); *last observation* is a still, darkened frame. Labels are always shown.

## Pacing

On automated play the seeds give roughly 60 minutes of simulated time at 1× (office ≈ 21, building ≈ 17, city ≈ 5, preservation ≈ 2, solar ≈ 2, distant offices ≈ 5, inventory ≈ 3, last desk ≈ 1.5), before reading and deciding. That is shorter than the manifesto's 90–150 minute hypothesis for chapters 4 and 5 in particular. The office and building were retuned at the owner's request (see 01 and 02); the other chapters keep their seeds, as the manifesto asks for tuning after observation rather than by adding currencies.

## Not implemented / open questions

- The original three chapter-one report variants were not supplied; three short factual variants were authored (all kept, some salvaged, all salvaged).
- The v1 office save format was not supplied; the importer accepts `{ version: 1, available, lifetime, upgrades, salvaged }`, reconstructs the uniquely implied wire use and machine mass, and rejects anything inconsistent.
- Background production of certified earlier chapters is summarized rather than simulated (their mass stays in their accounts until the terminal audit).
